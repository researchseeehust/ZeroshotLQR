01 Overview

Reward-free data, once. Any LQR task, instantly.

Classical RL and adaptive dynamic programming for the linear quadratic regulator solve one performance index at a time: change the weights $(Q,R)$ and the whole policy iteration must be rerun on stored, or freshly collected, data. We bring zero-shot reinforcement learning to LQR: for linear systems with quadratic costs, the successor features of every linear policy are exact quadratic forms, and a fixed-size second-moment statistic of reward-free data is a sufficient representation to solve every task — exactly, without touching the data again.

01

Exact structured successor features

Every linear policy's successor features are exact quadratic forms, and the map from a task $(Q,R)$ to its Q-function kernel is a closed-form, task-linear successor operator $\mathcal{L}_K$ — no neural approximation needed.

02

Two-phase zero-shot algorithm

An offline phase compresses arbitrary reward-free trajectories into a fixed-size representation; a test-time phase maps any task — weights or noisy reward labels — to a gain via a data-free policy iteration, or with zero iterations via successor-feature GPI.

03

Provable guarantees

Noise-free PE data give exactly Riccati-optimal gains for all tasks at once; SF-GPI is exponentially stabilizing and never worse than the best library policy; sub-Gaussian noise gives a uniform, data-computable suboptimality certificate.

04

Validated on an unstable batch reactor

Machine-precision exactness over 200 random tasks from one dataset; test-time cost of $0.4\,$ms independent of data length, versus $14$–$359\,$ms for per-task off-policy Q-learning; a continuous-time extension and robustness to noisy reward labels.

02 Method

One reward-free dataset, compressed once; any task, mapped in closed form.

The offline phase accumulates second moments of reward-free, off-policy trajectories and, optionally, a small library of successor operators for anchor tasks. At test time, any task — revealed as weights $(Q,R)$ or through reward labels — is mapped to a stabilizing gain by a data-free policy iteration in representation space (Mode PI), or with no iteration at all by successor-feature generalized policy improvement over the library (Mode GPI).

Theorem — uniform zero-shot guarantee

Under sub-Gaussian process noise, with probability at least $1-\delta$ — from a single probabilistic event, with no union bound over tasks — the gains returned by Mode PI are stabilizing and their relative suboptimality is bounded, uniformly over a compact task set $\mathcal{W}$, by $O\!\big(\sigma_w\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/(N\lambda_{\mathrm{PE}})}\big)$. A data-computable certificate, evaluated from the representation alone, verifies stability and suboptimality for each individual task.

03 Results

Machine-precision exactness, constant test-time cost, robust finite-sample rates.

On Rosenbrock's open-loop-unstable batch reactor ($n=4$, $m=2$), a single reward-free dataset solves 200 random LQR tasks to machine precision; SF-GPI closes most of the optimality gap with no test-time equation solving; and the predicted $1/N$ sample-complexity rate is confirmed across three excitation levels.
